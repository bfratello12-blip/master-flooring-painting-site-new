What we paint

Everything on the outside of the house

Most exterior calls start with "the front looks bad." Usually the whole envelope is due — and doing it together is cheaper than doing it twice.

  • Siding and shakes. Wood, cedar, fiber cement, aluminum and vinyl — each one gets the prep and the coating it actually needs.
  • Trim, soffits and fascia. The parts that fail first. Scraped back to sound material, primed and coated so they stop peeling every other spring.
  • Shutters, doors and garage doors. Small surfaces that change the whole front of a house when they're crisp again.
  • Porches, railings and columns. High-touch, high-wear surfaces finished in coatings made to take foot traffic and weather.
  • Decks and fences. Washed, brightened and re-stained or sealed — see power washing if that's all you need.
The prep sequence

Why the same house gets repainted every four years

Because somebody sprayed a coat over dirt, chalk and loose paint. Here's the order we work in instead.

01

Wash

The whole exterior gets cleaned of pollen, salt, mildew, algae and chalking. Paint doesn't stick to any of it, and you can't see the real condition of the surface underneath until it's clean.

02

Scrape & sand

Loose and failing paint comes off and edges are feathered so the new coat lays flat instead of telegraphing every old chip.

03

Repair, caulk & prime

Rotted trim addressed, gaps and joints caulked, bare wood and stained areas spot-primed. This is where water gets stopped before it gets in.

04

Coat properly

Quality exterior coatings applied at the right spread rate, in the right conditions, with the number of coats the surface actually needs — not the number that fits the schedule.
