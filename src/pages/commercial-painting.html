Property types

Spaces we paint on Long Island

Commercial work has a different set of priorities than a house: downtime costs money, surfaces take more abuse, and the finish has to hold up to cleaning.

Offices & professional suites

Open work areas, private offices, conference rooms, reception and corridors — painted evenings or weekends so Monday looks different and nothing else does.

Retail & restaurants

Sales floors, dining rooms, back-of-house and restrooms. Scheduled around service hours, with washable finishes where hands and carts hit the wall.

Apartment & rental turnovers

Fast, consistent unit repaints between tenants, including patching, trim and closet interiors. Good for landlords and property managers with a vacancy clock running.

Common areas & hallways

Lobbies, stairwells, corridors and laundry rooms in multi-unit buildings — the highest-traffic surfaces on any property and the first thing residents notice.

Commercial exteriors

Storefronts, façades, trim, awnings and railings. Washed, prepped and coated so the building doesn't look neglected from the parking lot.

Build-outs & new tenants

Fresh drywall, ceilings, doors and frames finished before a new tenant moves in — coordinated with whoever else is still on site.

How we keep you open

Downtime is the real cost of a commercial paint job.

The paint isn't what's expensive. Closing early, blocking a hallway or shutting a floor for three days is.

  • After-hours and weekend work. Evenings, overnights and Sundays when that's what the space requires.
  • Phased scheduling. Sections completed and reopened in sequence so you're never fully shut down.
  • Contained work zones. Floors and equipment covered, areas sealed, walkways kept clear and safe.
  • Low-odor products. Chosen for occupied spaces so staff and customers aren't dealing with fumes.
  • Durable, washable finishes. Specified for the traffic each surface actually sees.
  • One point of contact. You talk to the owners directly about scope, timing and changes.
