Why the finish holds up

Most of the job happens before the paint does.

Anyone can roll a wall. The reason a paint job still looks sharp five winters later is what happened underneath it — the patching, the sanding, the caulking, the primer, and the discipline to wait for the right conditions.

  • Real prep. Nail pops, cracks, peeling and water stains get addressed and primed — not painted over and forgotten.
  • Your house stays protected. Furniture is moved and covered, floors are papered, hardware comes off, and edges are cut by hand.
  • Quality coatings. We use durable, well-known products chosen for the surface and the exposure, not whatever is cheapest that week.
  • Clean at the end of the day. Tools packed, trash out, floors swept. You should be able to use your home while we're working.
  • You deal with the owners. The same people who quote your job are on it. Questions get answered directly.

Flooring & power washing

Floors laid flat and tight. Exteriors brought back to clean.

We install and remove flooring and wash exteriors across Long Island every week — vinyl plank, laminate and tile, full tear-outs, house washes, decks, patios and driveways. Both trades get a written estimate, a scheduled crew and a finish we put our name on.

  • The subfloor decides everything. We check for flatness, soft spots and moisture before a single plank goes down, then plan the layout so seams and cuts land where they should and every doorway gets a proper transition.
  • Tear-out that stays contained. Carpet, tack strip, old tile, adhesive and vinyl come up with the work area sealed and the dust controlled — and the debris leaves with us instead of sitting in your driveway.
  • Pressure is a judgment call. Vinyl, cedar, fiber cement, composite decking, bluestone and pavers each want different pressure, tips and distance. We soft wash where forcing it would etch, gouge or drive water where it doesn't belong.
  • Sequenced with the rest of the job. If painting is happening too, the trades go in the right order — walls first, floor after, baseboard and touch-ups last — so nothing gets damaged or done twice.

How a job runs

Four steps, no guesswork.

You'll know what's happening, who's showing up and what it costs before anyone opens a can.

Walkthrough
