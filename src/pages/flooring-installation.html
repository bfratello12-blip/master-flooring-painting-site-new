What we install

Durable floors that suit how the room gets used

Every material has a place. What matters is matching it to the room, the subfloor and the traffic — not to whatever's on sale.

  • Luxury vinyl plank. Water-resistant and hard-wearing, which makes it the usual answer for kitchens, basements, mudrooms and houses with dogs and kids.
  • Laminate. A cost-effective way to get a wood look through living areas and bedrooms, with a tough wear layer on top.
  • Tile. Bathrooms, entryways, laundry rooms and backsplashes — set straight, spaced evenly and grouted clean.
  • Transitions and trim. Thresholds, reducers, quarter round and baseboard handled so the edges look finished, not improvised.

Removal & prep

The old floor comes out the right way

Tear-out is the messy part, and it's where most of the headaches on a flooring job come from.

01

Controlled demolition

Carpet, tile, laminate, vinyl and the staples, tack strip and adhesive under them come up with dust kept down and the rest of the house protected.

02

Subfloor check

Once the old floor is off we look at what's underneath — soft spots, squeaks, height differences, moisture. You hear about anything we find before the new material goes down.

03

Clean install & haul-away

Layout planned so the seams and cuts land where they should, then the debris leaves with us. No pile in the driveway waiting for a dumpster.

Doing both at once

If you want painting too, we'll sequence it correctly.

When a room is getting a new floor and a new color, running them together means one setup, one protection phase and one cleanup.

It also gets the order right: walls and ceilings first, floor after, then baseboard and touch-ups last. That's how you avoid roller spatter on a brand new floor, or a baseboard that got painted before it was reinstalled. Because it's the same crew, there's nobody to coordinate between and no second contractor to wait on.
